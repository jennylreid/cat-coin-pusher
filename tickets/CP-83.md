## 1. Towers that fall (top priority)
**Why they slide today:** a tower gets one tip, only if the pile is moving it fast. If that tip doesn't lean it past 14
degrees, it stands back up and is never tipped again, so the pile slides it along until it goes over an edge whole.
**Tower fall: Topple** (Developer switch in Engineer tools; Today is the default; any board, levels included):
- a tower the pile has shoved past its limit gets a tip at its top (twice today's push) every 0.4 s while it is still
  upright and still moving;
- it leans to 48 degrees (or 0.7 s past 14) before it comes apart;
- it comes apart the way the old Board 3 terraces did: the coins keep the tower's spin and spread out, and the spill
  recording plays.
Nothing else in the physics, the pusher or the frozen pickup changes. Nothing falls by itself: with no drops for 30 s,
every tower still stands under both settings.

| Level (towers) | Today: fell in 60 drops | Topple: fell in 60 drops (burst on the board / went over an edge) |
|---|---|---|
| 2 (2) | 0-1 | 2 / 0, at about 8 s |
| 5 (5) | 1-2 | 4-5 / 0-1, between 11 and 22 s |
| 6 (5) | 1-2 | 5 / 0, between 10 and 25 s |
| 8 (5) | 0 | 4-5 / 0-1, between 9 and 25 s |

Heavier towers (3x, same look), tried for Jenny's "different size coins", were no better: slightly more went over an edge
whole. Bigger coins would mean resizing the approved coin art, so I haven't tried size: **Jenny's call** whether to reopen it.

## 2. Timing
**Today it already matters, but you can't see it.** Measured on Level 3, 2 coins a stroke, 30 strokes x 3 seeds: drops made
while the pusher is about halfway through its push (35-60% of its cycle) send 87-97 pieces over the front per 100 coins.
Random timing sends 48-61, and drops made while it's fully back 38-45. **Smallest change:** a light strip on the pusher's
front edge turns green during that window, and a drop inside it says "Good timing!" (at most every 1.5 s). The physics
doesn't change. Developer switch: Engineer tools -> **Timing strip: Off (default) / On**; when On it shows in every round,
levels included.

## 3. Aim pieces (stand-in art drawn in code), with a drop budget and the normal extras

number of drops, shown where the clock was ("18 drops"). Coins you win still go to your wallet. If the budget runs out and
the goal isn't met within 4 s, the round ends as "out of coins".

## Where to find it
Settings -> Developer -> Engineer tools -> **Board lab** (at the top of Engineer tools). Tap a board: Settings closes and a
round starts on it right away, with the cat you're on (its side game and power as usual). **Off (normal levels)** puts normal
play back. The choice also ends when the app is closed. With Off, play is byte-for-byte UAT 102's (checked on 3 levels).
- The goal is in the goal row above the cat's head: "LAB · <board name>", then the goal and its count (e.g. "Get 5 mice 2 / 5").
- When the goal is met, the round keeps going for 2 s so the spill plays out, then YOU WIN.
- A lab round never passes a level, unlocks a recipe or moves your level. It does pay coins like any round.
- No bonus prizes or cat sky-drops on lab boards (Level 1's quiet rules), so only the board's own pieces are in play.
- Approved art, coin art, pusher, physics and the frozen pickup are unchanged. The red coins are a tinted copy of the coin
  face drawn in code, used only on the Red coins board.

## The six boards
Bot numbers: drops (one coin every 0.4 s) until the goal was met, from the real game in desktop runs. Every board was won
with coins left, in under 60 s of its 150 s.

| Board | Goal | Trick | Why it might be fun | Bot drops |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tall tower** | Topple the tower | A 15-coin tower near the front, the tuna on top. Push it over the edge (or tip it). | One big moment: tower, tuna and a pour of coins at once. | 38-46 |
| **Cliff edge** | Get all 3 (cheese, shrimp, tuna) | A two-layer coin ledge at the front with the cheese perched on it. Shrimp and tuna sit further back on their own sides. | Each prize has its own lane: drop down its lane. | 45 (each prize's lane); 67-100 otherwise |
| **Bowling pins** (my idea, replacing the staircase) | Knock down all 6 pins | Six 5-coin pins in a triangle near the front, head pin in the middle lane. | A straight push takes several over together: a strike. Echoes your pinsetter idea. | 53-69 |
| **Break the wall** | Get the 3 prizes behind the wall | The levels' 5-tower wall. Yarn, bell and duck wait behind it until a tower breaks. | Breaking through, then the prizes rush out. | 110-196 (the long one) |
| **Mouse hunt** | Get 5 mice | 7 mice: 2 on short towers, 2 by the open sides (can be lost), 3 in the pile. If none are left on the board, one drops in. | Collecting a set; picking which mouse to go for. | 84-87 |

staircase of towers never fell like dominoes; it took 101-166 drops. Bowling pins uses the same push for a better moment.
