Jenny: "Lazy waltz with big droopy flops on the beat sounds great." Then: "Ragdolls need to flop."

## Trigger
Same as the Tuxedo and the Orange: the Dance meter (4 prizes) or the music-note coin, on the Ragdoll's own level boards
(Pet the cat mode).
- Pet the cat and the Dance break never overlap; each waits for the other.
- The Hug can't start during the dance. A dance that comes due during the Hug waits and starts after it.

## Rhythm
A slow waltz in 3/4 at 96 BPM, after a 3-beat count-in. 6 bars (11.3 s), 11 taps. Beat 1 is always the flop.
1. FLOP . .
2. FLOP . .
3. FLOP step step
4. FLOP . .
5. FLOP . lift
6. FLOP step step

The window is ±0.16 s and the tap offset still learns. A tap on a rest is off the beat; tapping every beat scores 7 hits and
11 misses. Perfect = all 11, which spins the wheel (Yawn, Jackpot).

## Moves (the Ragdoll's approved resting mitts and faces only; no new art, no fades)
- **The flop (signature move, every beat 1):**
  - Before each flop, both mitts float up.
  - On the beat, the whole cat flops: the mitts fall limp, splay out and droop (the mitt on the slump side falls furthest),
    with a small floppy rebound. This gives the Dance break's usual coin jolt.
  - The head slumps far to one side (about 32°) and sinks, alternating sides each bar.
  - The eyes switch instantly to the Ragdoll's own closed eyes (its CP-75 art, as in its petting squint).
  - It hangs limp, then drifts back up slowly through beats 2 and 3, eyes open again.
- **Steps (beats 2 and 3 in bars 3, 5 and 6):** one mitt dips, left then right.
- **All the way through:** a lazy side-to-side sway.

## Music candidates (Developer > Engineer tools > "Ragdoll dance music"; default A)
All three are made in code by me (CP-101) with the game's synthesizer, like the Tuxedo's and Orange's music. No samples, so
the game may use them freely. Each is in F major, the tune plays the taps, and every flop has a soft thud.
- **A: Music box.** Bass on 1, soft marimba "pah-pah" on 2 and 3, a plucked music-box tune.
- **B: Lazy oom-pah.** A round bass and a soft kick on 1, quiet square chords on 2 and 3, a square-wave tune.
- **C: Sleepy harp.** A rolling plucked arpeggio on every beat, bass on 1, a marimba tune.

I can't hear them here. Jenny picks on her phone, in game.

## Art note (Jenny's call; nothing changed)
- **A limp, flopped-over body:** the game draws the cat as a head and two mitts, with no body painting. The flop is shown by
  slumping the head and dropping the mitts, which reads clearly in the stills. A truly flopped-over pose (the cat lying on
  its side, eyes closed) would need one new painted piece: a Claude stand-in template, then Codex paints it.
- **Fluffier mitts:** they'd help the Ragdoll read as a Ragdoll, but the dance reads without them, because the flop is
  carried by the motion. The Ragdoll's mitts are recoloured copies of the approved frozen mitts. Fluffier ones would need
  Jenny to reopen the Ragdoll's mitt art, then one painted piece (resting mitt; the happy mitt only if she wants it to
  match) on the same canvas, so the motion code stays the same.
- **Fluffier face:** the same answer. It's a frozen recolour of the approved head art. A fluffier Ragdoll head (longer cheek
  and chin fur) would mean reopening each Ragdoll face it uses (resting, Sweet, closed eyes), one piece at a time.
  It's parked for Jenny.

## Phone test (UAT 128)
1. On the Ragdoll's board, fill the Dance meter or catch the music-note coin. "DANCE BREAK!", a 3-count, then a slow waltz.
2. Every beat 1 is a big, limp flop with closed eyes; the eyes open as it drifts back up. Steps dip one mitt.
3. It's learnable in a few tries, and a perfect dance (all 11) spins the wheel.
4. Pet the cat and the Hug never run together with the dance.
5. Engineer tools > "Ragdoll dance music": try A, B and C (each takes effect from the next dance). Say which one to keep.
6. The Tuxedo's and the Orange's dances are the same as before.
