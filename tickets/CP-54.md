## 1. Which sound plays when a dropped coin lands
**On the board** (`release.html` line 15066): the first time one of your coins touches down, the game calls `C46.land()` (line 40744). It skips the landing if the last one was under 0.09 s ago. Otherwise it picks 1 of 5 sounds at random and never plays the same one twice in a row. **Yes, more than one sound rotates in:**
- `coin-land.mp3` (1 in 5). It's the same file as `dragon-studio-dropping-a-coin-478359.mp3`: the MD5 matches the copy inside the UAT 70 JSON. It plays through `recordedSfx` (line 8255) at volume 0.25 with no pitch change, and is cut at 0.84 s. If the recording hasn't loaded, the code-made `steelAt` clink plays instead (line 6725).
- The 4 code-made CP-46 "drop on pile" sounds, `coin-2a`–`2d` (4 in 5; line 40709). Each one is:
  - a falling 170–215 Hz sine "thud";
  - a strike of 5 pure sine tones (1350–7810 Hz, 30–90 ms);
  - a neighbour strike or two;
  - a settle.

**On the title screen:** `pgSfx('coin')` → `sfx.pg('coin')` (line 39824) → `steelAt(t, 0.35×v, pitch 1–1.4, no settle)` (line 6341). No recording is used.
- The word dropping in: 7 clinks, 0.09 s apart, at v = 0.8 (line 39971).
- The coin pile-up (Developer "Title coins" 4): one clink per coin that lands, at v = 0.35, at most one every 0.07 s (line 39993). The default title rain (mode 2) makes no landing sounds, only the 7 letter clinks.

**Why it may sound like rain:** this is my reading of the code; I haven't heard it.
- The title clink and 4 of the 5 board landings share the same core: 5 pure sine tones from 1350 Hz that ring for 30–90 ms. That makes a pitched "plink", like a drop.
- The board drops also put a falling low sine under it, a "plop".
- The recording starts with a dull 600 Hz knock, and its main clink comes about 0.35 s later.
- Rain coins and gold coins clink on their first landing (`C46.hit`), from the same family of pure tones. That's outside this ticket.

**Level finding:** measured as the loudest 100 ms above 250 Hz, today's recorded landing is **12.9 dB louder** than the 4 code-made drops it rotates with. So 1 landing in 5 jumps out.

**How F is built:**
- Each landing picks one of A's takes and one of 4 pour slices, never repeating either.
- The pour starts 12 ms after the strike with a 12 ms fade-in, so it's a rustle rather than a second hit. It sits 9 dB under the take.
- Each slice is 0.2 s with a 90 ms fade-out, so at 4 taps a second (0.25 s apart) the pours don't run into each other.
- The 4 slices are normalised to the same level, so the layer is even. Take and pour each get the ±6% pitch variation.
- F is matched to A: −37.14 against −37.19 dB on the loudest 100 ms above 250 Hz, averaged over all 12 take-and-pour pairs.
- Peaks: 0.17 holding and 0.20 tapping, the same as A (0.17).

**E, as UAT 59 played it** (checked in `work/cp59/release.html`. The PM says the Oct 31 production build played it the same way; I checked only UAT 59):
- In UAT 59 every landing called `sfx.land()` (line 15056) → `recordedSfx` `land` (line 8245):
  - the same file (MD5 matches in the UAT 59 and UAT 70 JSONs);
  - volume 0.25, cut at 0.84 s, no pitch change;
  - skipped if under 0.09 s after the last landing;
  - a pool of 3, so a 4th overlapping landing restarts one that is still playing.
- The page copies all of that, including the pool (at most 3 at once, even when tapping 4 a second). E is **not** level-matched: it plays at its real level, about 13 dB above the code-made drops and A–D. It sounds louder partly for that reason.
- Not copied: the game's cap of 6 recordings at once, which the mouth-drop sound shares. In play, that cap skipped about 39% of UAT 59's landings (CP-46). So the real game had gaps the page doesn't.
- E's title pile-up is the same steel clink as Today's. The title screen came in with UAT 59 and has always played that clink, so there's no earlier title sound to restore.

**Variation and levels:**
- Like the game, the page skips a landing under 0.09 s after the last one, and never repeats a variant.
- A–D vary pitch by ±6% each time. That's the range of the game's own `steelAt` clink.
- The game doesn't vary a landing's volume, so neither does the page.
- A–D are matched to the code-made drops, the level CP-46 set against the music: about A −0.7 dB, B +1.0, C +0.7, D +5. The gains are measured each time the page loads (D's varies by about 0.5 dB because it's random) and shown at the bottom.
- In a 6 s hold run, peaks are 0.10–0.26 (today 0.26). Nothing clips.
- The title pile-up plays candidates at the game's title-to-board ratio, (0.35×0.35)/0.8, so it is quiet, as it is in the game.

## Limits

- Sound on a PC is not sound on a phone. A phone speaker drops the low end, so D and the "plop" in today's sound will differ there.
- I used only recordings already in the project folder. If none of A–C is right, phase A could add new CC0 or Pixabay "coins on a pile" recordings. Downloading them needs Jenny's OK.
