# CP-46 New music and sounds: audition report

## Round 2: whoosh-only pusher (Nov 14)
Jenny's round 1 picks: no new music (the current music stays); all coin sets approved, to play in rotation; none of the 5 pushers. She wants "just a whoosh", one per stroke, loud enough to set a beat for the board, with no drums or ticks.

Each option has two files in `tickets/CP-46-evidence/audition/`: the whoosh alone, and the same whoosh `-under-music` (over the current Classic music at Music volume 100%). Every file follows the game's timing: 3 normal strokes (3.2 s each), 4 Turbo strokes (1.5 s each), then 1 normal stroke, about 19 s. Each stroke is a forward whoosh followed by a softer return whoosh.
- `pusher-r2-0-current-for-comparison` (+ `-under-music`): the pusher as the game plays it now, with its beat layer, at the 50% default.
- `pusher-w1-soft-whoosh`: one smooth, mid-toned whoosh per stroke; the return is softer and a little lower.
- `pusher-w2-airy-whoosh`: lighter and brighter, more air than body, with a slightly longer tail.
- `pusher-w3-sweeping-whoosh`: rises as the pusher goes forward ("whooo-sh") and falls on the way back.
- `pusher-w4-quick-whoosh`: a shorter "fwoosh" at the start of each stroke, so the beat is crisper.
- `pusher-w5-warm-whoosh`: rounder and darker; the return is only a faint breath.

Validation (`audition/levels-round2.json`, generator `work/cp46-audition/make-round2.cjs`):
- No drums, clicks or ticks. Each whoosh is filtered noise with slow fade-ins. The steepest 10 ms rise in loudness is 0.15–0.28 of the peak, against 0.89 for the current pusher's beat hits.
- Level: every whoosh sits 10 dB under the music where a phone speaker plays (averaged over the file). That is about 13 dB louder than the current pusher at 50%, so it carries the beat. The game's Pusher sound dial only goes up 6 dB, so the build will set a new base level and the dial still trims it. Mixes peak at 0.59, so there is no clipping.
- Turbo makes the whooshes shorter and slightly higher, matching the faster strokes.
- The current pusher and the music in the mixes are rendered from the game's own code (UAT 54). The music bus limiter is left out because it doesn't act at these levels.

## Round 1 listening guide
Files are in `tickets/CP-46-evidence/audition/` (OneDrive). Nothing is in a game build. Each music file loops cleanly if your player repeats it.

**Music (pick any you like; the beat is what the Dance break would tap to)**
- `music-A-pizzicato-caper.wav`: plucked strings tiptoeing, a bouncy bassoon, woodblock tick-tock on every beat. Cheeky, sneaky cat. 132 BPM, 29 s.
- `music-B-sunny-ukulele.wav`: strummed ukulele, marimba tune, a whistle joins at the end, claps on 2 and 4. Sunny and easy. 112 BPM, 26 s.
- `music-C-bossa-cafe.wav`: nylon guitar bossa nova with a breathy flute. Calm café; the softest beat of the six. 132 BPM, 29 s.
- `music-D-toy-box-pop.wav`: music-box tune, toy piano, glockenspiel sparkle, steady kick, a meow at the end. Cute, close to Kawaii pop. 128 BPM, 30 s.
- `music-E-funky-strut.wav`: clavinet, slappy bass, horn stabs and a horn tune. Groovy and syncopated. 100 BPM, 29 s.
- `music-F-candy-pop.wav`: a new tune in the Kawaii pop direction: pumping chords, bell lead, "meow" chops. Bright and danceable. 136 BPM, 28 s.

**Coins (sets of variants so the board doesn't repeat one sound)**
- `coin-0a-current-land…`, `coin-0b-current-clink…`: what the game plays now, for comparison.
- `coin-1a`–`1d-clink`: coins knocking coins on the pile (short, damped; some double taps). For coin-on-coin contacts.
- `coin-2a`–`2d-drop-on-pile`: a coin dropped onto the pile: dull hit, ring, neighbours chiming, a little wobble. For a dropped coin landing.
- `coin-3a`–`3d-spill-3/4/5/6-coins`: several coins going over the edge into the tray. For small payouts.
- `coin-4a`–`4c-big-cascade`: a whole row pouring over (about 25 to 40 coins, 1.4 to 2.2 s). For big pushes, rain and Quake.
- `coin-5a-gold-drop`, `5b-gold-over-edge`, `5c-gold-clink`: heavier coin with a long ring and a shimmer. For gold coins.

**Pusher (each file: 3 normal strokes, 4 Turbo strokes, 1 normal, at the game's timing)**
- `pusher-0-current-for-comparison.wav`: the current pusher at its 50% default.
- `pusher-1-soft-glide.wav`: a smooth felt swish, soft wooden "tok" at the end. The quietest feel.
- `pusher-2-ratchet.wav`: light clicking gear under the travel, small clunk at the end; clicks speed up in Turbo.
- `pusher-3-air-glide.wav`: soft pneumatic "fsshh" that rises then settles, "pff-thk" at the end.
- `pusher-4-wooden-drawer.wav`: grainy wooden slide and a hollow "tock".
- `pusher-5-tick-tock.wav`: a soft tuned "dum" as it sets off, woodblock "tik" where it stops; Turbo goes up a 4th. The most musical.

`index.html` in the same folder has players for all of them (opens in a browser on the PC).

## What was done
- Audition only. No build, `uat/`, `phone/` or `work/cp55` touched. The frozen pickup sounds (ledge bell, landing thump) are left alone; these coin sounds are for plain coins, gold coins and pile/edge contacts, which have no pickup sound.
- Generator: `work/cp46-audition/make-audition.cjs` (`node work/cp46-audition/make-audition.cjs`). It uses only the Web Audio calls the game already makes (oscillators, gain envelopes, one biquad filter, looped noise) and the CP-38 offline renderer, so any pick can go into the game the way Kawaii pop did (UAT 41: the audition code copied in and played on the audio clock).
- Music styles were chosen not to repeat the CP-38 round (lo-fi, chiptune, disco, swing jazz and tropical were heard and not picked), except Candy pop, a new tune in the Kawaii direction she liked best. All original tunes over common chord progressions; nothing imitates a known song.

## Validation (numbers in `audition/levels.json`)
- Loudness: all six tracks at the same level where a phone speaker plays (above 250 Hz): 6 dB over one coin landing, the level Classic and Kawaii pop play at Music volume 100%. Peaks 0.16–0.46, no clipping. Bass sits an octave up with a strong 2nd harmonic, so nothing relies on deep bass.
- Loops: each file is the second of two rendered loops (it starts with the first loop's tails); the jump from last to first sample is 0.0002 or less.
- Beat: an onset check at each beat. A, B, D, E: 98% of beats clearly marked; F 95%; C (bossa) 73%, its beats still 2.5× stronger than the off-beats. E (funk) is busy on the off-beats by design; its kick and snare stay on the beat.
- Coins: each set scaled as a group to the current coin landing (loudest 100 ms above 250 Hz): clink and drop-on-pile 0 dB, gold +2, spills +3, cascades +6; variants keep their natural differences (±1 dB).
- Pusher: every option scaled to the same loudness as the current pusher at 50%, so they compare fairly.

## Limits
- Rendered offline, not heard on the phone; Jenny judges by ear. Mono, 44.1 kHz.
- The pusher demos are on their own; how each sits under the music is easiest to judge by playing a music file and a pusher file together, or after a pick in a UAT build.
