# CP-74 Hug: can the current arm art "unfold forward"? Plain answer (Nov 18, UAT 104 rebuilt, NOT published)

**Channel note (21:14):** the board-lab engineer has since published CP-83 round 2 as **UAT 104** (numeric 20261118000015,
the number my staged build used). My `work/cp104` package is now stale and will never publish: `publish.ps1` refuses because
the baseline changed. Whatever CP-74 version Jenny approves gets rebuilt on the then-current UAT, as the next number (check
`work/cp105`, which another engineer has started). The fail in my last frozen-pickup run was only its "UAT channel untouched"
check catching that publication; its other 9 checks passed.

**Short answer: not well, and new art won't fix it on its own either.** The arm sits in the thin strip between the board and
the screen edge (about 110 units wide at the top, 24 at the front corner), and the painted arm already fills that strip.
- Any bent pose of a rigid upper arm and forearm puts the forearm and mitt either off-screen or over the board, where it is
  clipped away because nothing may cover the play area.
- So the paw can't visibly lead: it only comes into view near the end.
- That holds for Codex's arm cut at the elbow (tried, below) and would hold for separately painted upper-arm and forearm
  pieces too.

**Tried (104c, today's art, no repainting):** I cut Codex's arm at a natural elbow point (canvas row 1000 of 2080) into an
upper arm (rounded end) and a forearm (top fades over the upper arm). It unfolds gently with ease in and out:
- the forearm and mitt open from the elbow (0.1-1.2 s);
- the upper arm swings down from the shoulder (0.3-1.4 s);
- walls go up at 1.4 s;
- letting go reverses it over 1.3 s.

Nothing stretches. You see the upper arm fold down from the shoulder; the forearm and mitt arrive only in the last part.
- Video with sound: [hug104c-412x915.mp4](CP-74-evidence/uat104/hug104c-412x915.mp4) · stills: [sheet104c-412x915](CP-74-evidence/uat104/sheet104c-412x915.png)
- Joint positions at 412x915 (game units): shoulder pivot (51, 380), elbow (14, 560), wrist/cuff at the held mitt
  (-5, 824). Canvas pixels on the 800x2080 arm canvas: shoulder (485, 280), elbow (335, 1000).

**Ways forward (Jenny's call):**
1. **Let the paw pass over the board's top-side corner during the reach** (about 1 s; it would briefly cover a few coins
   near the back corners). Then the cut-at-elbow arm can fold properly: the paw starts tucked under the cheek, reaches
   forward over the corner and settles in the lower corner. Code only, today's art. Cheapest; I can video it quickly.
2. **New pose art** (how 2D games show a reach toward the camera): per side (left painted, right mirrored), extra
   paintings of the same arm in the same fixed 800x2080 canvas and registration as revision 2, so the held pose is unchanged.
   - **Tucked:** shoulder plus a short bent upper arm, the wrist just under the cheek, about (60, 470).
   - **Half reach:** elbow bent, forearm coming toward the player, foreshortened, wrist about (20, 650).
   - **Held:** today's painting.
   - Each painting ends at a wrist cuff, so the game places the approved mitt there, moved and turned but never resized.
     Each pose comes with its wrist point and angle.
   - The game swaps the poses with short crossfades and a little motion. This still has to fit the strip, so the half
     reach mostly reads as the arm bending, not travelling.
3. **Change the layout** to give the arms room (for example a slightly narrower board on screen). That changes the locked
   layout, so it isn't recommended.

My recommendation: try 1 first (no new art, about an hour). Use 2 only if Jenny wants a real foreshortened reach.

Also noticed: the "BONUS PRIZE!" text from a timed bonus drop can show during the Hug. CP-82's hold covers callouts only,
not that text. It affects every power, not just this change; it's a separate small fix if Jenny wants it.

# CP-74 Hug, step 1: layout prototype (Claude engineer, Nov 18)

This is a prototype to watch, not a release. I didn't touch `uat\`, `phone\` or any release file. It's built on UAT 91
(`work\cp91\release.html`, pinned by hash), and the work is in `work\cp74-hug\` and `tickets\CP-74-evidence\`.

## What it does (about 7 s in all; the sides are blocked for exactly 6 s)
1. **0 s.** "HUG!", a meow, the paw whoosh and Pet the cat's "mrrp". The idle mitts lift away behind the head.
2. **0–0.35 s.** The Ragdoll's **shoulders** come forward at the head's lower corners, behind the head.
3. **0.12–0.45 s.** **Thick furry arms** grow down both sides of the board. They are fur-coloured, with the cream side
   toward the board and fur tufts on the outer edge.
   - Each arm's inner edge sits on the table edge, so the arms are the gutter walls.
   - The walls are today's two Hug colliders, same size and place. They go up at 0.45 s, when the arms reach the front,
     and come down at 6.45 s, when the arms start to let go. What blocks the sides is what you see.
4. **0.35–0.6 s.** The forearms curl in under the front lip. The **approved mitts** slide in from the bottom corners and
   meet below the board.
   - The mitts are the same images at the same size (182) as the idle mitts, drawn the same way. They are only moved and
     turned in 0.3 rad, so the claws point toward each other.
   - At the clasp there's a burst of 36 hearts and Pet the cat's "content" trill.
5. **The hug (6 s).**
   - **Purring:** the Pet the cat purr engine (`sfx.purr`), steady the whole time.
   - **Hearts:** about 16 a second rise from the arms and mitts, and 6 a second pop up anywhere over the board.
   - **The mitts knead,** gently and in turn.
6. **Let go (6.45–7.05 s).** The mitts slide out, the arms pull back up, the shoulders sink back and the idle mitts drop
   back into place. The purr fades out.

**Nothing covers the play area.** The shoulders and arms are clipped to outside the table outline. The mitts sit below
the front lip (the visible palm starts at y 778, and the lip is at 771). They're drawn under the coins, so coins and
prizes going over the front edge, and the frozen pickup, stay in front of them. Only the hearts and today's
"NO SIDE FALLS" words cross the board.
