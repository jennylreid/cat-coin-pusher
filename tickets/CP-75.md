## What was wrong
The wheel's Yawn (CP-58) always drew the Tuxedo's Runway yawn face (`cp58-yawn-face.webp`) and squinting eyes
(`cp58-yawn-eyes.png`), whichever cat's board was playing. On the other cats the yawn turned their head into the
Tuxedo's for about 1.4 s, and while eating, the eye art's two black patches (the Tuxedo's fur behind the squint) sat on
their face like black eyes.

## How the other cats' heads are made, and what I did
The other cats aren't separate paintings. The game recolours copies of the Tuxedo art when it runs (`pgRecolor` in
`release.html`, "placeholder cats"). Grey pixels become the cat's fur→light colour by brightness, and coloured pixels
(yellow eyes, pink ears, nose and tongue) stay as they are. The Tortie gets a second fur colour from a fixed sine pattern.
I made each cat's yawn the same way. `work/cp75-yawn-faces/recolor.py` is a port of `pgRecolor` with `PG_CAT`'s colours,
run on the approved Tuxedo yawn face and eyes:
- `art/cp75-yawn-face-{orange,ragdoll,siamese,tortie}.webp` and `art/cp75-yawn-eyes-{…}.png` are full 1536x1024 on the same
  canvas, so they have the same size and place as the Tuxedo yawn and nothing moves. The eye patches are now the cat's
  own fur colour. The Tortie's patches follow the same pattern as its normal head, so they line up.
- I added one thing to the recolour: the open mouth (dark inside, its outline and the line up to the nose) is kept as
  painted. Recoloured, it would have turned fur-coloured (an orange mouth on the Orange).
- Prototype: `proto.html` is `work/cp94/release.html` (UAT 94 source) plus the block `cp75-yawn.js`. The block swaps
  in the copy for whichever cat's head art is on screen (it hooks `pgApplyArt`/`pgRestoreArt`). The Tuxedo keeps the
  approved originals. Nothing else changes: timing, mitts, pickup, sounds and the normal faces are all the same.
- Files are smaller than the original face (430–500 KB lossless vs 688 KB; eyes about 21 KB each).
