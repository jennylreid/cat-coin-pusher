## Step 1b report: cleaned, game-ready versions (Nov 18)
Jenny on A, B and C: "A and B are the best, but they're not great for a game… The CodeMade sound at least is clear. The
other ones are scratchy." These versions start again from the full-quality originals (`work/cp79-yawn-sound/originals/`).
There's no UAT build, and nothing else in the game changed.

**Watch these** in `tickets/CP-79-evidence/`. They're the same real-game Yawn at 412x915 as before, 3.1 s, with the sound
at 0.48 s: `yawn-sound-A2.mp4`, `yawn-sound-B2.mp4`, `yawn-sound-AB.mp4`, and `yawn-sound-today.mp4` (unchanged). The
plain audio is beside them: `yawn-sound-{A2,B2,AB}.wav/.mp3` and `yawn-sound-today.wav`.

| | What it is | Length |
|---|---|---|
| **A2** | A's close-up yawn squeak, cleaned: a falling yawn (about 0.56 → 0.41 kHz). Raised 2 semitones to be a little cuter, with a faint clean breath | 1.21 s |
| **B2** | B's kitten yawning mew, cleaned: a high, sweet mew sliding down (about 0.81 → 0.65 kHz) | 1.05 s |
| **AB** | B's mew for 0.85 s, gliding into A's falling squeak ending (raised 5 semitones, so it continues from the kitten's pitch) | 1.52 s |
| today | The code-made glide, unchanged | 1.65 s |

**How they were cleaned** (`work/cp79-yawn-sound/polish.py`). Filtering alone can't remove the phone hiss in A, which
sits all over the voice. So I measured each cat's own voice from the recording every 5 ms: its pitch line and the strength
of each harmonic. Then I rebuilt the sound from those harmonics only. Everything between the harmonics is gone: hiss,
phone noise, B's background hum, handling clicks.
- The pitch movement and timbre are the cat's own.
- I fixed tracking slips: no octave jumps, and gaps where the voice was too weak to measure are filled from neighbouring
  frames.
- A's natural dip mid-yawn is lifted so the sound stays continuous.
- Polish: upper harmonics slightly up (brighter, sweeter) and the lowest three +2–2.5 dB (fuller).
- All four files have the same loudness (RMS −20 dBFS over the sounding part; peaks −8 to −10 dBFS, none clipped).

**My honest view.** By the spectrograms, all three are now as clean as today's: steady, clear harmonics with no noise.
Unlike today's they move like a real cat (the uneven pitch wobble and the real harmonic balance). B2 is the best bet: the
kitten's mew is the cutest and most "game" sound, and B was the cleanest recording to start from. A rebuilt voice can
sound slightly whistly or electronic, and I can't hear whether it does. So it's possible Jenny still prefers today's for
clarity. If she does, a middle option is today's code-made glide reshaped to follow B2's pitch line.

**In the game (Step 2):** it would replace only `sfx.wild2('yawn')`, at the same moment. To match today's in-game
loudness, play the file at about 0.35 (−9.1 dB): today's sound runs at −29.1 dBFS RMS against these files' −20.

## Step 1 report: 3 candidates (Nov 18)
Engineer: Claude (in the "CP-75 Yawn faces" conversation, as the brief allows). Step 1 only: no UAT build, nothing in

**Today:** the Yawn's sound is code-made (`sfx.wild2('yawn')`): a 1.65 s sawtooth that slides 420→560→240 Hz through a
sweeping filter. That's why it sounds fake.

**Watch these** in `tickets/CP-79-evidence/`. Each is the real game's wheel Yawn (UAT 95, Tuxedo board, 412x915), 3.1 s:

  levelled to the same loudness.

| | What it is | Length | Source | Licence |

**How they were made:** `work/cp79-yawn-sound/`.
- `make_audio.py` trims each recording, applies a gentle high-pass (to remove handling rumble and hum) and short fades, and
  levels them to the same loudness (RMS −20 dBFS, peaks ≤ −1 dBFS).
- `clips.cjs` renders the frames and muxes the clips. The game's other sounds (chomps, NOM.) aren't in the clips, only the
  yawn sound.
- Sources are in `sources/`. They're Freesound's public preview MP3s (128 kbps), downloaded with Jenny's OK.

**Searched and not used:**
- Freesound 327582 "Cat Yawn" (CC BY 3.0): too faint and distant (about −60 dB) with hum.
- Freesound 383473 "Cat purring and yawning" (CC0, 1:46): purring throughout, with no clear voiced yawn I could find.
- Pixabay: "cat yawn" returns only human yawns and plain meows.
- Freesound 854551 "Yawning Kitty": made in a synth, not a real cat.
