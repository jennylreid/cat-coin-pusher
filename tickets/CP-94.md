## Trigger (PM OK, Nov 19)
Same as the Tuxedo. On the Orange's board, the Dance meter fills with prizes (4), or all at once from the music-note coin.
Treat toss keeps its fish coin and CP-88 slot. The two never overlap: each waits for the other, and the slot refuses a tap
during the dance. Only on the Orange's own level boards. The old Developer "Treat toss" mode and the other cats are
unchanged.

## Rhythm
108 BPM (the Tuxedo's is 109, but the Orange taps on half beats). The dance is 4 bars (8.9 s) after a 3-beat count-in, 19 taps:
1. Steady: dun, dun, dun, dun (1 2 3 4)
2. dun, dun, dun-dun-dun (1 2 3&4)
3. **The pounce**: 1, &2!, 3, 4. One tap off the beat, the surprise.
4. **The scramble**: dun-dun-dun, dun-dun-dun (1&2, 3&4)

Judging is as forgiving as the Tuxedo's. The window is ±0.15 s and the tap offset still learns.
- A tap is judged against the nearest tap, so quick taps get the full window.
- A tap on a rest is off the beat.
- Tapping on every half beat doesn't pay: it scores 8 hits and 24 misses.
- Perfect = all 19 taps, and it earns the wheel (Yawn, Jackpot) like the Tuxedo's.

The lane shows only the taps.

## Moves (the Orange's approved art only: resting mitts; Sweet, Sharp Grin and Wild faces; no new art, no fades)
- **Swipes** on the beats: big alternating mitt swipes (one presses down and in while the other lifts). The head leans
  and wobbles. The wobble is fixed per beat, so it looks chaotic but is the same every time.
- **Quick runs** (dun-dun-dun, the scramble): both mitts climb in steps and wiggle, one higher than the other. The face
  switches to the **Sharp Grin**. The mitts drop a beat after the run, which gives the Dance break's usual coin jolt.
- **The pounce**: both mitts slam down together, the head ducks, and the face switches to the **Wild** face for that
  moment.
- Faces switch instantly.

## Music candidates (Developer > Engineer tools > "Orange dance music"; default A)
All three are made in code by me (CP-94), the same way as the Tuxedo's beat. No samples or outside recordings, so the
game may use them freely. Each one's tune plays the tap rhythm note for note, so the song teaches the dance. F major,
ending on an F chord.
- **A: Alley rock.** A gritty driving bass, kick, snare, loud hats and a plucked lead. Claps and a cowbell on the quick
  runs; a crash and a tom on the pounce.
- **B: Wild mambo.** A clave on every tap, congas between, a shaker and a cowbell, with a marimba lead. Timbale hits climb
  through the quick runs; a crash on the pounce.
- **C: Ginger chip.** A fast chiptune: noise drums, a bouncing triangle bass, a busy arpeggio between the taps and a square
  lead. Rising blips on the runs; a crash and a high blip on the pounce.

I can't hear them here. Jenny picks on her phone, in game.
